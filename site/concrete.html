The story in five scenesThe romance is invented. The record is not.
01
Before her time

The ex who tried harder

Her predecessor in HD 73, Kyle Biedermann, carried quarry legislation in 2019 that residents called far more comprehensive. The neighbors remember. Every Hallmark heroine is haunted by a previous relationship.

02
March 2023

The gentle touch

She files two bills. HB 3658 would require aggregate and concrete batch operators to monitor emissions at their fence lines. HB 3624 would give priority to operators with reclamation plans. Her statement: "I believe these businesses can co-exist with their neighbors."

She doesn't want to change him. Just watch him from the property line.

03
March 2023

The neighbors aren't swooning

The spokesman for the coalition fighting the Vulcan quarry calls her bills "very tiny steps in the right direction," and says they leave "lots of wiggle room for big polluters." What they wanted, he says, was legislation with "some enforcement teeth."

04
2023 – 2024

He gets the ring

The Texas Supreme Court declines to review the neighbors' challenge to Vulcan's air permit. The state's environmental agency approves the water pollution abatement plan, which opponents describe as the second and final permit Vulcan needs to start mining over the Edwards recharge zone. Vulcan says it will operate "in a safe, socially and environmentally responsible manner."

05
June 2026

Paving paradise

Hill Country counties put two concrete-shaped items on their legislative wish list: county authority over impervious cover, and tougher permits for aggregate mining. Every new slab is rain that doesn't reach the aquifer. Local coverage doesn't list her among those setting the priorities.

Her side of the aisle

A growing Texas needs homes, roads and schools, and those need rock. Monitoring and reclamation are reasonable steps that don't shut down a lawful industry. Vulcan says the site supports local infrastructure and road safety.

The neighbors' side

Quarries don't belong in non-industrial areas over the aquifer's recharge zone. Opponents say this one brings dust, truck traffic and risks to wells and water quality, and that fence-line monitoring without enforcement changes nothing.

The series finale

Water, servers, rooftops, rock. Four loves. One ballot.
