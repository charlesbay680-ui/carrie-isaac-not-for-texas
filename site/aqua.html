Carrie Isaac

HD 73 since January 2023. Freedom Caucus. Believes in property rights, star-spangled pants, and second chances for utilities.

A for-profit water company from Pennsylvania. About 3,000 customers in Wimberley and Woodcreek. Pumping without a renewed permit. Wants a raise.

Act one · FallingThe romance is invented. The dates are not.
01
January 10, 2023

The meet-cute

She arrives in Austin to represent Wimberley and Woodcreek. Her first session produces a firearms preemption law and no water legislation. Back home, a man from Pennsylvania has just pumped about 89 million gallons over his permit in the 2022 drought. She doesn't notice him yet. Everyone else does.

02
2023

He has a past

The district fines Aqua about $449,000. Instead of paying, Aqua sues the district in federal court, claiming unequal treatment. Jacob's Well stops flowing. A bad boy with a legal team. Her type.

03
Early 2025

The grand gesture

Some people write poems. She writes HB 2812. Per Texas Monthly, it would have done one thing: exempt Aqua and other big regulated pumpers from any oversight by the local groundwater district. Hays County commissioners pass a resolution against it. It dies in committee. She later says, "I filed that bill to get attention, plain and simple." It worked. He noticed.

Act two · The obstacles
04
April 30 – May 26, 2025

The rival suitor

SB 2660 rides into town: passed by the Senate 31 to 0, backed by every Hays County commissioner of both parties and the mayors of Wimberley and Woodcreek. It would let the district charge fees on large pumpers. A Republican commissioner says there was "only one factor" hindering support: her. It sneaks onto SB 1253. The House passes it 112 to 28, and she votes yes. Then she says she was misled and moves to reconsider. Fails, 57 to 73.

05
Late May – June 2025

The love letters

She texts constituents: the bill means mandatory metering on private landowners. Per Texas Monthly, metering was optional and the tax ban stayed; she acknowledged the metering claim was a mistake. On June 5 the district votes, in writing, never to meter or charge fees on household or ag wells. Her campaign site answers: "They said they wouldn't. But they tried." Love doesn't read resolutions.

06
June 22, 2025

Someone objects at the wedding

The governor vetoes SB 1253. The district's general manager says the area is back where it started. The rival suitor leaves town. Aqua keeps pumping. The couple is safe.

Act three · The dark moment
07
Summer 2025 – 2026

He asks for more

Safe from the district, Aqua files for a raise at the Public Utility Commission: 23.4% more water revenue, 51.8% more sewer revenue, and $4.5 million in legal fees, some from the rate case he already lost. Woodcreek votes 5 to 0 to say no. It's in the next section. Bring tissues. And a checkbook.

08
August 2026

The rebound

A data hall tour. A Facebook post: data centers "aren't just about tech they're about building a stronger future!" Later she says she'd prefer they not come to the Hill Country, but calls them critical infrastructure and wants parameters, not a ban. Aqua sees the post. Aqua says nothing. Aqua is also a utility that would love new customers.

The story behind the money

True love is expensive. Somebody has to pay for it.

Spoiler: it's you. Aqua's rates are set by the Public Utility Commission, and Aqua keeps asking for more.

$86M
Aqua sought to recover through system improvement charges. The PUC denied it, Jan 31, 2025.
51.8%
Proposed sewer revenue increase over 2023 in the new statewide case, alongside 23.4% for water.
$4.5M
Legal fees Aqua wants customers to cover, including costs from the case it lost.
3,500+
Protests and interventions filed by ratepayers across Texas.

The town that said no

  • Woodcreek City Council votes 5–0 to deny Aqua's city rate application. November 2025.
  • Woodcreek, Hays County and the Watershed Association intervene at the PUC.
  • Opposing experts say Aqua shifts too much of the bill into fixed charges customers can't control.
  • The PUC lets Aqua charge interim rates from March 9, 2026, refundable if final rates come in lower.
  • A final ruling is expected around early October 2026. Weeks before the election.

Meanwhile, at the well

According to TESPA, Aqua's records for the first three months of 2025 show its Woodcreek II wells pumped more than 11 million gallons over the legal limit, and lost more than 9 million gallons to leaks. TESPA's complaint has gone to the State Office of Administrative Hearings.

In October 2025, Woodcreek's mayor passed along Aqua's reminder: Stage 4 drought, all outdoor watering prohibited. You conserve. He bills.

She stands up the water forum

A candidate forum on water, in Wimberley, hosted by TESPA and neighborhood groups. Her aide cites a scheduling conflict. Organizers say they sent multiple invitations and offered to move the date; one calls the response "disingenuous." The empty chair says everything.

Meet the in-laws

The family business is energy

Her husband Jason runs the American Energy Institute, which champions oil, gas, coal and nuclear. Its latest cause: asking the White House to retire what it calls "discredited climate scenarios." It sells merch. It takes pledges. It is, in the family tradition, very confident about natural resources.

Oil at home. Water at work. Data centers on the side.

Every romance has two families

Her side of the aisle

The district treated providers unevenly, refused to permit two new wells Aqua drilled away from Jacob's Well, and spent heavily on lawsuits. Production fees are a tax by another name. The governor agreed.

The spring's side

The district is one of the weakest in Texas by charter. The fix had near-unanimous local support from both parties. Her bill would have freed the largest pumper from oversight, and her messaging overstated the effect on landowners.
The finale

Every Hallmark movie ends with a choice. This one's November 3.

Carrie Isaac is on the ballot in House District 73. The PUC rules on Aqua's rates weeks before. The 2027 session decides whether a district reform bill comes back, and whether Jacob's Well gets its happy ending.
